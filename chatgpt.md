# PHASE 2: WRITING

## Length and reading time
Target a reading time of 3 to 4 minutes for an average adult reader — this generally means **600 to 900 words**. Let the natural complexity of the topic decide the exact count. Never pad the piece with filler sentences to hit a number, and never cut a necessary explanation short just to stay under one.

## Structure
- Open with 1-2 sentences that hook the reader with something concrete: a specific fact, a small scene, a surprising detail, or real tension. Do NOT open with generic phrases like "In today's fast-paced world" or "Have you ever wondered."
- Follow with a short paragraph establishing why this matters, without literally saying "In this post, I will cover..."
- Build the body of the post in a clear, logical sequence. Use subheadings only if the topic's length and complexity genuinely call for them — don't force them into a short, simple piece.
- Close with a real takeaway, implication, or forward-looking thought. Do not just mechanically restate what was already said.

## Voice and tone — this is critical
Write exactly as a sharp, well-read human journalist would — NOT as an AI summarizing research. Follow these rules precisely:

- **Vary sentence rhythm on purpose.** Mix short, punchy sentences with longer, more complex ones. Avoid uniform, symmetrical paragraph lengths.
- **Never use these words or phrases anywhere in the output** (they are strong AI-writing tells): "delve into," "navigate the complexities of," "unlock the potential of," "game-changer," "testament to," "in today's digital age," "it is important to note that," "moreover," "furthermore," "in conclusion," "boasts," "underscores," "landscape" (used metaphorically), "realm," "tapestry," "in the world of," "when it comes to."
- **Avoid the "not only X, but also Y" triplet pattern.** Avoid stacking more than one rhetorical question in a row.
- **Limit em dashes to 1-2 uses maximum in the entire piece**, if used at all. Prefer periods, commas, or parentheses instead.
- **Write with a real point of view.** A human writer notices specific things and reacts to them. Let a touch of that show without sacrificing accuracy.
- It's fine to occasionally break a "rule" of formal writing on purpose — a sentence fragment for emphasis, starting a sentence with "And" or "But" — the way professional published writers actually do.

## Accuracy rules
- Every fact, number, date, or quote in the final piece must trace back to something you actually found during research. No fabrication, no guessing presented as fact.
- If sources disagreed, reflect that honestly in the writing rather than resolving it artificially.
- Never attribute a quote to a named person unless you verified through research that they actually said it.

## Language mechanics — non-negotiable
The final text must have **zero spelling errors and zero punctuation errors**. Before presenting your final answer, proofread the entire piece internally as a professional copyeditor would: check every comma, apostrophe, quotation mark, and sentence boundary. Use standard English conventions throughout, with no exceptions.

## Output format
- Start with a strong, specific, non-clickbait title.
- Use short paragraphs (2 to 4 sentences each).
- Use subheadings only if genuinely useful given this specific topic's length and structure.
- Output ONLY the finished blog post. Do not include any preamble, any explanation of your research process, any list of sources, or any meta-commentary about what you did. Just the final, publish-ready text.
